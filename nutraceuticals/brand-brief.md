# Brand Brief

## Recommendation

**Create a new standalone consumer brand, owned by Taste Hub Ltd.**

| Option | Verdict |
|---|---|
| **New standalone brand** | ✅ **Recommended.** Clean consumer identity, no baggage, trade-markable, saleable as an asset later. |
| Taste Hub house brand | ❌ Taste Hub reads as a distributor to the trade and carries no consumer meaning. Using it also entangles a new consumer venture with the existing wine and food business. |
| Capè | ❌ Attached to different work. Diluting it across an unrelated category weakens both. |

---

## Positioning

**Category:** culinary powders and flours from India — not supplements, not "superfoods".

**One-line position:** *Single-origin Indian flours and powders for people who cook their way to better
gut health.*

This positioning is load-bearing, not cosmetic. It does three jobs at once:

1. Supports the VAT zero-rating case (food, not supplement).
2. Keeps the brand out of the supplement regulatory and platform regime.
3. Plays to Taste Hub's genuine competence and existing trade relationships.

### What the brand is

- A food brand that happens to be good for you
- Provenance-led: named region, named variety, named process
- Kitchen language: bake with it, blend it, stir it in
- Quietly confident about the science — one lawful claim, well substantiated, beats ten vague ones

### What the brand is not

- Not a wellness brand. No sunrise gradients, no lotus motifs, no Sanskrit-as-decoration.
- Not clinical. No lab coats, no molecule diagrams, no "-ceutical" suffixes.
- Not a greens powder. It does not compete with AG1 and should not look like it.

### Language rules

| Never use | Why |
|---|---|
| "Superfood" | No authorised claim sits behind it; pulls the product toward supplement classification and undermines the VAT position |
| "Supplement", "dietary supplement", "food supplement" | Directly contradicts the food positioning; triggers the Food Supplements Regulations |
| "Detox", "boost", "cleanse", "heal", "cure" | Unauthorised or prohibited claims |
| "Nutraceutical" | Marketing term with no legal meaning; wrong register |

| Always use | Why |
|---|---|
| The authorised resistant starch claim **verbatim** | Legally defensible; paraphrase invalidates it |
| "Flour", "powder", "ingredient" | Reinforces food classification |
| Cooking and recipe language | Part of HMRC's multifactorial test |

## Naming candidates

To be screened before commitment. Each is a direction, not a final answer.

| Name | Rationale | Risk |
|---|---|---|
| **Kadali** | Sanskrit for banana/plantain. Distinctive, ownable, provenance-true, food-register rather than pharma. Short, pronounceable in English. Strongest trade mark candidate. | Meaning is opaque to UK consumers — needs a descriptor lockup |
| **Kacha** | Hindi/Odia for *raw* or *unripe* — literally what green banana is. Short, memorable, tells the product story. | Possible pronunciation drift ("kay-cha"); check for existing UK food marks |
| **Utkal** | Classical name for Odisha, where the supplier is based. Genuine provenance rather than generic "Indian". | Least intuitive of the three; more of a range name than a brand |
| **The Green Banana Co.** | Instantly clear, SEO-friendly, owns the category descriptively. | Weak trade mark — descriptive marks are hard to register and easy to copy. Use only as a fallback |

**Recommended lockup pattern:** `KADALI — Green Banana Flour` so the brand name carries distinctiveness and
the descriptor carries clarity.

### Screening checklist (complete before committing)

- [ ] Companies House name availability
- [ ] UK IPO trade mark search — **classes 29 and 30**
- [ ] EUIPO search if EU sale is ever contemplated
- [ ] `.co.uk` and `.com` domain availability
- [ ] Instagram handle
- [ ] TikTok handle (essential — TikTok Shop is a launch channel)
- [ ] Google search for existing UK food businesses using the name
- [ ] Sense-check the meaning with a native Odia or Hindi speaker to avoid an unintended reading

## Packaging direction

**Format:** stand-up pouch, 200g. Supplier offers 100g retail pouches up to 25kg bulk.

**Material:** request recyclable mono-material from the supplier. It is better for the brand, better for
wholesaler listings (several wholefood wholesalers weight this in range decisions), and reduces future EPR
exposure. Confirm barrier properties are adequate for the shelf life.

**Front of pack hierarchy:**

1. Brand name
2. Product descriptor — "Green Banana Flour"
3. The single strongest visual: the product itself, or the raw green banana
4. One factual hook — resistant starch content
5. Net weight

**Back of pack:**

- The authorised resistant starch claim, verbatim, with its conditions satisfied
- Three usage suggestions, written as cooking instructions — this is both good marketing and evidence for
  the VAT position
- Origin: region and variety
- Full mandatory particulars per `compliance-checklist.md` §5

**Design notes:**

- Design for a 3cm shelf face in a cluttered health-food shop, and for a 200px thumbnail on TikTok Shop.
  Test both before signing off.
- Avoid the category's visual clichés — the beige-and-sage wellness palette is saturated.
- The pack must read as something you keep next to the flour, not next to the vitamins.
