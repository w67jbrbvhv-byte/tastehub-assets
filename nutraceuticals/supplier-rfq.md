## 1. Gate Zero — organic certification (blocking)

Your catalogue lists FSSAI, ISO 22000, HACCP and GMP. It does not name an organic certification body, and
only one product ("Organic Green Banana Powder") is described as organic. To sell any product as organic in
Great Britain we need the following. Please answer each point directly.

1. **Certificate scan.** Your organic certificate showing: certification body name, body code, scope
   (which products, which operations), issue and expiry dates.
2. **Is your processing and packing site itself certified organic**, or are only the farms certified?
   Both are required for a retail-ready organic pack sold in GB.
3. **Can your certification body issue a GB Certificate of Inspection (COI)** for each consignment shipped
   to Great Britain, endorsed by them?
4. If you are not currently certified: **are you in conversion, with which body, and what is the expected
   certification date?**

> **Buyer-side check (do not send):** verify the named body against Defra's GB register at
> `gov.uk/government/publications/organic-registers-lists-of-third-countries-or-territories-control-bodies-and-control-authorities`.
> Bodies recognised for India include INDOCERT, IMO Control (GB code XX-BIO-147), OneCert and Control Union.
> **An NPOP or APEDA certificate alone is not sufficient for Great Britain** — GB maintains its own
> recognition list and has removed Indian bodies that lost APEDA accreditation. If the body is not on the
> GB list, the product cannot be sold as organic here regardless of what the Indian certificate says.

## 3. Priority products — specification request

Our lead interest is your **green banana range**, not the herbal/Ayurvedic lines. Please quote on:

| Ref | Product | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| A | Green Banana Flour (fine) | Primary SKU |
| B | Green Banana Powder (raw) | Secondary SKU |
| C | Organic Green Banana Powder | Only if Section 1 clears |
| D | Turmeric Powder | Phase 2 consideration |

For each, provide a full technical data sheet including:

- **Resistant starch content as a percentage of total starch** — critical, see note below
- Total starch, dietary fibre, protein, moisture content
- Particle size / mesh
- Banana variety and growing region
- Processing method (air-dried, spray-dried, freeze-dried) and drying temperature
- Shelf life and storage conditions
- Allergen statement and cross-contamination controls (the facility handles sesame and tree nuts per your
  catalogue — declare this explicitly)
- Whether the product is raw / unheated, and whether it is treated with any sterilisation step

> **Why resistant starch matters:** GB retained health-claim law (Reg. 432/2012 as amended by 2016/854)
> authorises the claim *"Replacing digestible starches with resistant starch in a meal contributes to a
> reduction in the blood glucose rise after that meal"* where resistant starch is **at least 14% of total
> starch**. This is one of very few lawful health claims available in this category and it is central to our
> proposition. We require the figure on the specification and confirmed by independent analysis.

## 6. Testing and quality — non-negotiable

Your catalogue states "in-house lab testing, third-party COA". We need to understand precisely which is which.

**Our requirement for every consignment**, tested by an **accredited third-party laboratory** (not in-house),
with certificates issued before shipment:

| Test | Reason |
|---|---|
| Ethylene oxide | The UK applies extra control measures to Indian imports for pesticide residues including ethylene oxide, following carcinogen findings. This is the single highest-risk parameter. |
| Heavy metals — lead, cadmium, arsenic, mercury | India is the largest single source of heavy-metal samples in FSA monitoring. |
| Pesticide multi-residue screen | Border control requirement. |
| Aflatoxins / mycotoxins | Dried plant commodity. |
| Microbiological — TVC, yeast & mould, E. coli, Salmonella, Listeria | Standard. |
| Resistant starch as % of total starch | Substantiates our on-pack health claim. |

Please confirm:

1. The name and accreditation of the third-party laboratory you use.
2. That you will provide batch-specific CoAs, not generic or historic ones.
3. That you accept payment terms contingent on passing results (Section 4.4).
4. That you will retain reference samples from each batch for at least the product shelf life.

> **Buyer-side note (do not send):** commission independent UK-side verification testing on the first
> consignment of each SKU and compare against the supplier's CoA. The two must agree. Budget £600–1,200 per
> consignment.

## 7. Products we are not pursuing

For clarity, we are **not** seeking quotes on the following, for UK regulatory reasons:

- **Ashwagandha** — under live FSA safety review with no published outcome; no established safe levels in
  the UK; banned in Denmark; unauthorised novel food for non-supplement uses.
- **Shatavari, Giloy, Brahmi, Safed Musli, Arjuna Bark, Bhringraj, Neem, Triphala** — GB novel food status
  requires individual verification and is unresolved for several.
- **Aloe Vera powder** — GB restrictions on hydroxyanthracene derivatives from Aloe leaf.
- **Green tea extract, curcumin extract** — extracts carry a different and heavier regulatory burden than
  powders.
