# UK Private Label Powders — Market & Go-to-Market Strategy

Prepared for Taste Hub Ltd | September 2026

---

## 1. Situation

Taste Hub Ltd (est. 2013, Southend-on-Sea) imports and distributes wine and food into wholesale,
convenience and specialist channels. The opportunity under assessment is a private-label range of
nutraceutical / superfood powders sourced from India, sold online.

Taste Hub starts with three assets most entrants to this category lack: an existing UK import operation,
customs and logistics competence, and live trade relationships with specialist retail buyers. The strategy
below is built to exploit those rather than to imitate a venture-funded D2C brand.

**Operating constraints:** no Amazon; retail-ready finished goods from India; under £25k for phase one.

**Nominated supplier:** MpLinn Foods, Odisha (catalogue reviewed — see `supplier-rfq.md`).

---

## 2. Market read

### Size and growth

The UK category is growing, but published figures need care. Technavio's widely-cited "USD 6.74bn growth in
UK superfood powders 2024–28" is not credible for a UK-only market and should not appear in any document
shown to a lender, investor or buyer. Defensible statements:

- Powdered formats are the fastest-growing form within dietary supplements, at high-single-digit to ~9%
  CAGR across independent sources.
- Consumer-recognised ingredients cluster around turmeric, moringa, spirulina, cacao, maca and baobab.
- Gut health is the dominant UK wellness theme and the one with genuine scientific and regulatory support
  behind it.

### Price benchmarks

| Product | Price |
|---|---|
| Naturya Organic Green Superblend | ~£13.98 / 250g |
| Rheal Superfoods Clean Greens | ~£25 / 150g |
| Single-ingredient organic powders (typical) | £10–16 / 100–250g |

### Channel dynamics

- **TikTok Shop is where the category is growing.** Vitamins and supplements became TikTok Shop's largest
  Health & Beauty category at $784m for the year to February 2026, ahead of facial skincare and fragrance.
  Functional food sales grew 265% year on year. The UK is TikTok Shop's most mature European market, with
  200,000+ business sellers.
- **D2C paid acquisition is unaffordable at this budget.** Supplement DTC CAC averages ~$89 with 5–8%
  monthly subscription churn. £25k of paid media buys roughly 250 customers and no inventory. Paid-led D2C
  is therefore excluded from phase one by arithmetic, not by preference.
- **Independent health-food retail is reachable cheaply** through the wholefood wholesalers, which serve
  roughly 450 UK health-food outlets.

## 3. The central strategic decision: position as food, not supplement

This single choice drives labelling, pricing, channel access and margin. It must be designed into the first
artwork because it cannot be retrofitted.

### 3.1 VAT — the largest single swing in the model

Food supplements are standard-rated at 20% VAT in the UK. Powders supplied and consumed in a form most
people would associate with food can be zero-rated. In **Global By Nature Ltd v HMRC [2025] TC09396** the
First-Tier Tribunal held powdered vegan food supplements to be zero-rated, rejecting HMRC's argument that
they were standard-rated sports drinks.

HMRC applies a multifactorial test: nature and description, ingredients, manufacturing process, size and
appearance, taste and texture, packaging, marketing, and circumstances of consumption. HMRC's own position
is that vitamins and minerals are not food for VAT purposes regardless of format — so the case rests on the
product genuinely being a food, not on wording alone.

**Consequence — and it lands differently by channel.** Modelled at the current assumptions
(`landed-cost-model.xlsx`, toggle the ZERO-RATED cell on the Inputs sheet):

| Channel | Zero-rated | Standard-rated | Effect |
|---|---|---|---|
| D2C (own Shopify) | £11.74 GP/unit, 87.7% | £9.14 GP/unit, 84.7% | **+£2.60 per unit** — the full 1/6 of the consumer price stays in the business |
| Wholesale | £3.10 GP/unit, 65.3% | £3.10 GP/unit, 65.3% | No direct effect — the trade price is quoted ex-VAT either way |
| Retailer margin at a £12.99 shelf price | 63.4% | 56.1% | **+7.3 points of headroom for the retailer** — which converts into either an easier listing conversation or a lower shelf price than a standard-rated competitor can match |

So the gain is direct on D2C and competitive on trade: it does not raise the wholesale margin, it makes the
product materially easier to list and to price against capsule and tablet competitors. Incorrectly claimed and
later challenged, the liability is 1/6 of all historic sales.

⚠️ **This position is fact-specific and HMRC litigates it actively. Obtain a written opinion from a
food-VAT specialist (~£800–1,500) before artwork sign-off.** Treat zero-rating as designed-for, not booked,
until the opinion is held. Retain it as a file note.

### 3.2 Regulatory surface

| Sold as a food supplement | Sold as a food |
|---|---|
| Food Supplements (England) Regulations 2003 | Retained Reg. 1169/2011 (food information) only |
| Must be labelled "food supplement" | No such requirement |
| Mandatory dose and warning statements | Not applicable |
| Trading Standards notification | Not applicable |
| Supplement-specific platform gates | Ordinary food category |

### 3.3 Platform access

Amazon requires third-party cGMP verification for all dietary supplements, with enforcement from
31 March 2026 — a serious obstacle for an India-manufactured product from a supplier founded in 2024.
TikTok Shop's supplement approval process is materially tighter than most brands anticipate. The food route
avoids the worst of both.

### 3.4 Fit with Taste Hub

A culinary powder sells into delis, farm shops, specialist convenience and wholefood retail — the buyers
Taste Hub already calls on. A supplement sells into a channel Taste Hub has no relationships in, against
incumbents with far deeper pockets. The food positioning converts an existing asset into a launch advantage.

## 4. Product strategy: lead with green banana flour

The supplier's declared signature range is green banana powder — raw, spray-dried, freeze-dried, fine and
coarse flour, starch and peel powder. Leading with it rather than another moringa or turmeric SKU is the
single largest improvement available to this plan.

| Rationale | Detail |
|---|---|
| **It is the supplier's actual expertise** | Best quality, price and process flexibility will be here. Buying their strength rather than their sideline reduces execution risk. |
| **A lawful, authorised health claim** | Green banana flour is approximately 65% resistant starch. Retained Reg. 432/2012 (as amended by 2016/854) authorises *"Replacing digestible starches with resistant starch in a meal contributes to a reduction in the blood glucose rise after that meal"* where resistant starch is ≥14% of total starch. The product clears that threshold with very large headroom. In a category where competitors can only gesture at benefits in unregulated wellness language, holding a lawful on-pack claim is a durable competitive asset. |
| **Unambiguously a food** | It is flour. Strongest possible VAT zero-rating case; no novel food question; no botanical safety review. |
| **Differentiated in UK retail** | Established in the US (NuBana, Jonnys Good Nature, MRM Nutrition) but thin on UK shelves — The Gut Tailor uses it only as one component of a blend. No UK brand owns the category. |
| **Not a commodity price fight** | Moringa and turmeric powders are a race to the bottom against dozens of sellers with identical product. This is not. |
| **Rides the strongest UK trend** | Gut health, with prebiotic fibre and resistant starch as the mechanism, is the most durable wellness narrative in the market. |

### Launch range

| SKU | Position | Phase |
|---|---|---|
| Green Banana Flour (fine) | Hero — baking and cooking, plus the resistant starch claim | 1 |
| Green Banana Powder (raw) | Smoothie and stir-in format, same source | 1 |
| Turmeric or masala blend | Familiar entry product, leans on Taste Hub's food credibility | 2 |

### Excluded on regulatory grounds

- **Ashwagandha.** The FSA opened a call for evidence on ashwagandha in food supplements on 8 July 2024,
  following safety signals from the WHO, Denmark's DTU and Germany's BfR regarding thyroid hormone effects,
  thyroid toxicity, hypoglycaemic effects and possible liver toxicity. No safe levels are established in the
  UK, no outcome has been published, and Denmark has banned it. It is additionally an unauthorised novel
  food for all uses other than food supplements — directly incompatible with the food positioning. Placing
  a launch SKU on an ingredient under live review is an avoidable single point of failure.
- **Shatavari, Giloy, Brahmi, Safed Musli, Arjuna, Bhringraj, Neem, Triphala.** GB novel food status must be
  verified individually; several are unresolved.
- **Aloe vera powder.** Verify against GB restrictions on hydroxyanthracene derivatives from Aloe leaf.
- **Green tea and curcumin extracts.** Extracts carry a heavier regulatory burden and pull the range back
  toward the supplement classification.

## 5. Channel strategy

Sequenced cheapest-acquisition-first. This ordering is what makes a £25k launch viable.

### Tier 1 — Trade (near-zero acquisition cost)

Taste Hub's existing relationships are the unfair advantage. Two routes:

- **Wholefood wholesalers:** Suma (largest independent wholefood wholesaler in the UK), Infinity Foods
  (Brighton), CLF (largest health and wellness wholesaler, 18,000+ SKUs), Tree of Life. These reach the
  ~450 UK health-food outlets without direct selling.
- **Direct:** delis, farm shops and specialist convenience from Taste Hub's own customer book.

### Tier 2 — Faire (UK)

No listing or monthly fee; application required. Approximately 15–25% commission on first orders from new
retailers and ~15% on reorders, but **0–10% via Faire Direct for retailers introduced by the brand** — so it
doubles as a free ordering and payment back-office for accounts Taste Hub already owns.

### Tier 3 — Own Shopify store

Brand home and full-margin channel. Seeded by organic content and the credibility of trade listings, not by
paid acquisition at scale. Paid social testing is a phase-two decision, gated on proven repeat purchase.

### Tier 4 — TikTok Shop UK

The one consumer marketplace worth entering in phase one: not Amazon, rewards content over ad budget, and is
the fastest-growing surface in this exact category. Requires FBO registration approximately 28 days before
selling, certificates of analysis, GMP evidence and compliant labels.

### Deferred

Amazon, Ocado, Holland & Barrett, Planet Organic — listing fees and volume commitments that £25k cannot
underwrite.

### On the decision to skip Amazon

The concern raised — Amazon observing a successful product and private-labelling it — is real but
second-order for a powder with no defensible IP. The stronger reasons to skip Amazon in phase one are
economic: third-party copycat listings, listing hijacking, price erosion toward the category floor, and the
cGMP verification gate applied to an India-manufactured product. The cost of skipping it is real — Amazon is
the UK's largest supplement search surface — and the decision is worth revisiting in phase three, once a
trade mark is registered and the range has a differentiated blend behind it.

## 6. Import and supply

### CETA — claim it

The India–UK Comprehensive Economic and Trade Agreement was signed on 24 July 2025 and **entered into force
on 15 July 2026**. Processed foods, spices, and fruit and vegetable preparations move to zero duty from
previous rates of 4–16%. Confirm the specific commodity code with a customs broker and **claim preferential
origin on every entry** — first-time importers routinely fail to, and it is a direct margin loss.

### Organic — unresolved

The supplier catalogue evidences FSSAI, ISO 22000, HACCP and GMP but **names no organic certification body**.
For GB sale as organic, the producer must be certified by a body on Defra's GB-recognised list for India
(INDOCERT, IMO Control, OneCert, Control Union and others); an NPOP or APEDA certificate alone is not
sufficient. Taste Hub would additionally need its own organic importer licence from a UK control body, and a
GB Certificate of Inspection endorsed by the supplier's body for each consignment. UK–India organic
equivalence remains under negotiation and must not be assumed.

**If organic cannot be evidenced,** the recommended response is to drop the organic claim and sell on
provenance, single origin and traceability. The hero product does not depend on organic certification, and
dropping it removes roughly £1,000/yr of cost and a category of border risk. Organic is a nice-to-have in
this proposition, not the proposition.

### Contamination — the principal business risk

The UK applies extra control measures to Indian spice imports for pesticide residues including **ethylene
oxide**, following carcinogen findings. India is the largest single source of heavy-metal samples in FSA
monitoring at 25% of samples taken. EU data flagged 365 Indian products for pesticides and heavy metals
between 2024 and 2026. Full Border Target Operating Model controls — documentary, identity and physical —
are operational across all risk categories.

A retail-ready container rejected at the border is a total write-off that a £25k business cannot absorb, and
the nominated supplier is an unproven exporter founded in 2024.

**Protocol:** accredited third-party pre-shipment testing on every consignment covering ethylene oxide,
heavy metals, pesticide multi-residue, mycotoxins and microbiology; independent UK-side verification on the
first consignment of each SKU; payment contingent on passing CoAs. Budget £600–1,200 per consignment. In-house
supplier testing is not acceptable as sole evidence.

### Retail-ready from India — managing the two risks

- **MOQ exposure.** Finished-goods MOQs typically run 3,000–5,000 units per SKU against 100–500kg for bulk.
  At £25k this caps the range at two or three SKUs. The supplier has not disclosed MOQs; the RFQ demands
  them, and offers a price premium for a reduced 2,000-unit first run against committed reorder volume.
- **Label errors become container write-offs.** Relabelling after landing is impractical. Mitigated by a
  formal pre-production gate: a printed proof approved in writing by both the regulatory reviewer and the
  VAT adviser before any production printing, plus photographs of the first filled units before shipment.

## 7. Unit economics

200g pouch. Figures are placeholders pending live RFQ data — the supplier has published no prices, and
public bulk quotes for Indian powders reflect low-grade conventional material, not certified retail-ready
goods.

| Line | £/unit |
|---|---|
| Green banana flour, retail-ready | 0.80 |
| Pouch, label, filling | 0.45 |
| Sea freight LCL + clearance | 0.25 |
| Duty (CETA preferential) | 0.00 |
| Testing, amortised | 0.15 |
| **Landed cost** | **~1.65** |
| Trade price to wholesaler | 4.50–5.00 |
| Wholesaler to shop | ~6.00 |
| Shop RRP | 11.99–13.99 |
| D2C RRP | 14.99–16.99 |

Gross margin per unit, as computed in `landed-cost-model.xlsx`: **65.3% wholesale (£3.10), 59.1% via Faire
(£2.39), 87.7% D2C (£11.74)** — assuming zero-rating. Standard-rated, D2C falls to 84.7% (£9.14) and the
retailer's margin at a £12.99 shelf price falls from 63.4% to 56.1%.

## 8. Principal risks

| Risk | Severity | Mitigation |
|---|---|---|
| Organic cannot be evidenced by a GB-recognised body | High | Gate Zero in RFQ; fall back to provenance positioning |
| Consignment rejected at border on contamination | Critical | Accredited third-party testing every consignment; payment on passing CoA |
| VAT zero-rating challenged by HMRC | High | Specialist written opinion before artwork; documented file note |
| Supplier failure (founded 2024, unproven) | High | Paid sample first; small first run; qualify a second source before scaling |
| MOQ exceeds budget | Medium | Negotiated 2,000-unit first run at premium unit price |
| Label non-compliance discovered after landing | Medium | Pre-production printed proof sign-off gate |
| Trade listings slower than modelled | Medium | Revenue lags listing by 6–8 weeks; hold contingency |
